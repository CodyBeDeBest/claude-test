An izakaya table, built around the sushi counter

East Izakaya keeps the format Tokyo's back-street izakaya have run for decades: low light, shared plates, a kitchen that stays loud until close. What we changed is the counter — a rotating, all-you-can-eat sushi menu that gets treated with the same care as an à la carte one, not a buffet steam table.

Nigiri is cut to order, rolls come out in small batches, and the robata grill keeps the rest of the room fed with skewers, sake, and the kind of small plates built for sitting a while. Come for the sushi. Stay past the second round.
